## 🎮 Live Demo

### [abdulahmad.github.io/NHL9XStudio](https://abdulahmad.github.io/NHL9XStudio/)

To host it yourself, in repo **Settings → Pages**, set **Deploy from a branch**, branch **`gh-pages`**, folder **`/ (root)`**. Pushes to `main` refresh `gh-pages` via the workflow. **NHL 92** runs from bundled sprite banks; for Genesis/SNES ROMs use **Open file…** in the header (or drag-and-drop). **Open folder…** loads an NHL 95/94 PC install.

NHL 92 pictures come from the `.ANIM` files in `ANIM-To-BMP`, or from the same bytes in a retail ROM when one is present. NHLPA 93, NHL 94, and NHL 95 Genesis read a player sprite sheet out of the ROM. The SNES ports read planar 4bpp tiles from their ROMs. Names come from each game's `frames.asm`: an **SPF** symbol is a run of frames, and an **SPA** entry is an 8-facing sequence of those frames. Genesis pixels are 4bpp column-major tiles, nibble 0 transparent, four Genesis palettes.

**Groups** sorts the list into skating, shooting, and the other sidebar categories. **File** keeps the order of the symbols in `frames.asm`.

Timing follows `updateanim`. A step of N vblanks stays on screen for N + 1 vblanks, because the vblank that expires the counter still draws the old picture. The game also refuses a new picture until 4 vblanks after the last change (`glitch`). **Game timing** applies both. A negative time is the last step, so the stride back to the first frame waits out that full hold. Flags bit 0 is the loop bit.

Facing 0 is north on a vertical rink (`dirtab` in `logic.asm`), then clockwise. A horizontal rink subtracts 2 from the facing before the lookup. Spin walks the compass where an animation actually has more than one sequence.

Pin holds one point in the middle of the stage. **0, 0** is the default: the player's position, which sits at the skates. **Hotspot** is the pair `GetHot` reads (file offsets `0x18` and `0x1A`), where the puck sits in possession. An amber mark is 0, 0 and a red cross is the hotspot. A dashed rectangle is the canvas size from the frame header (`0x06` and `0x08`, stored as twice the pixel size). On these banks that size matches the sprite bounds.

## Run

```
node server.js
```

Open http://localhost:8732

- **Frame** scrubs a bank by index. `Sprites.anim` frame 1 is docs index 0. Use **Bank** on NHL 92 and on NHLPA 93 / NHL 94 Genesis to switch the player sheet, crowd, face-off sprites, zamboni, and other frame banks (NHL 94 also lists the playoff sprite sheet). SPF and SPA names still refer to the main player sheet only.
- **SPF** shows one named run. Runs laid out as eight facings get the compass.
- **SPA** plays a sequence. Q and E turn the facing, S spins.
- **Images** lists the other pictures in that game: titles, the rink, fonts, and the animation banks that are not the player sheet. Each Genesis and SNES picture pixel keeps its palette row (color plus row times 16). Color 0 of palette 0 is drawn. Color 0 of the other rows is transparent, so a tile on palette 1, 2, or 3 can sit on top of the ice instead of a black box. The Transparency checkbox on Images starts checked when the picture uses those holes, and clear when the picture is opaque. Turning it on or off overrides that for the picture you are looking at. The tab opens on the picture's own palette. Choosing a team in the jersey menu recolors that picture with the team colors, which is how the scoreboard, jumbotron clips, team blocks, and the fonts pick up a club's colors. A picture made of several frames stays on the first one until you press Play, and the filmstrip lists every frame of that picture. SPF and SPA still start playing on their own. Switching games stays on Images. A picture with the same name stays selected. Otherwise the app returns to the picture you last opened for that game during this session. Below 1100 pixels wide the list and the details slide off the stage; **List** and **Details** open them again. NHL 94 SNES pictures are the title letters, the title goalie, the title puck, the EA Sports screen and its backdrop, Ron Barr, the Stanley Cup, the window frame, the team logos, the rink, the center-ice marks, the face-off box, the team blocks, the EASN logo, the ref windows, the big and small fonts, the letter sets, the face-off sprites and the Zamboni, and the 160 player cards. The face-off box, EASN logo, fonts and ref windows are maps the game keeps unpacked in ROM, drawn over their packed tiles. The rink pictures and fonts take the game's own `RinkPalette` from the ROM, not the bundled NHLPA 93 `Rink.snespal`. The card bytes are expanded by the same routine the roster screen uses (`$9B85C2`). NHLPA 93 SNES also draws the face-off box, the framer, the arrows, the team blocks, both ref windows, and the horizontal rink. The EASN backdrop uses the Ron Barr colors loaded with that screen. The frame and the big font use the ice palette after the same pixel remap the game uploads. NHL 92's frame does the same remap onto the ice-rink colors. NHLPA 93, NHL 94, and NHL 95 Genesis list every picture the disassembly extract scripts name: screens, the rink, fonts, team logos, player portraits, and the crowd, faceoff, and zamboni frame banks. A picture with no palette of its own uses the ice-rink colors. The crowd scale slider zooms from that fitted size. The **Revision** picker next to Game lists the retail revisions: NHLPA 93 Rev 0 / A / B and NHL 92 Retail / Rev A. NHL 94 and NHL 95 Genesis, NHL 94 and NHLPA 93 SNES, and NHL 95 PC each had one retail release, so they have no picker. Those offsets are the retail ROM. An NHLPA 93 ROM whose header date is `1992.OCT` (Rev A) shifts every slice at or after `$16E53` by `0x2E` bytes, which covers the pictures and the player sprite sheet. A ROM dated `1993.FEB` (Rev B) is placed by `lib/rev-b.js`: EA changed the EASN branding to EA Sports, so the pictures that carried it are recompressed and sit at their own offsets (the spinning EASN animation is gone and `EASports.map.jim` / `EASports2.map.jim` replace the EASN bitmaps), and the sprite offset tables have no entry for frame 0 (`tableBias`). The Audio tab reads the NHLPA 93 sound streams at the revision's own offsets (Rev A is `0x2E` later, Rev B is 12 earlier); the Sound test, instruments and sequencer use the bundled retail sound segment whatever ROM is loaded. NHL 96 is not a supported game; its extract script names palettes, not pictures. NHL 95 PC still lists every QFS picture.
- **Audio** has the same sections in every game that has them: Songs, SFX, Samples (Genesis DAC PCM files, SNES BRR blocks) and Instruments (Genesis FM patches, SNES instrument entries), named by the game's own IDs: 92 `SngEOP`, 93G `$30 · SngGoal`, 93S `4 · SngGoal` (the songs are numbered by their in-game cue, 1-9, and the title and menu songs are in the same list as the game ones). The headings come from `lib/audio/categories.js`. On NHLPA 93 SNES a sample or an instrument plays as one probe note of the instrument on the game's own sound program (a sample plays through the first instrument that uses it). The Images tab uses the same groups and order for NHLPA 93 and NHLPA 93 SNES; the SNES rows list the files of the disassembly (`IceRink.chr.pak + IceRink.smap`).
- On NHLPA 93 SNES the ice rink and the horizontal rink open on a team palette (home colors in palette row 2, the team's visitor colors in row 3, as the game fills them), so the benches and the scoreboard show team colors.
- NHLPA 93 SNES lists the same pictures as NHLPA 93, plus its sprites (Face Off sprites, Zamboni, Crowd) in the Sprites group. The energy bar is three tiles of the small font (red, yellow and green, drawn in palette row 4); the EASN logo is `EASN.smap` on `RinkLogoTiles.chr.pak`. The "Hockey Night" bitmap of NHLPA 93 has no SNES picture: the game prints that line as text. The SNES pictures take their colors from `data/pals/snes/Rink.snespal`, which the local server and the static site both serve.
- Jersey colors replace the home-jersey palette. That is slot 2 on the Genesis games and on NHLPA 93 SNES, and slot 4 on NHL 94 SNES. The menu uses the city and home or away, and it opens on Buffalo home so the jersey zones are all visible. Embedded stays in the menu for games whose own palette is trustworthy; on the Genesis games the menu also lists the four palette lines stored in the ROM or ANIM file (`Embedded palette · line 0` to `line 3`), each used as the jersey line, because the default line is not always a usable jersey. NHL 92 uses the `ANIM-To-BMP` palettes. NHLPA 93 and NHL 94 Genesis use the `ANIM94-To-BMP` palettes. NHL 95 Genesis uses `ANIM95-To-BMP/Palettes/NHL95`. Copies of all of them are bundled in `data/pals/genesis` (NHL 92, NHLPA 93, NHL 94) and `data/pals/genesis95`, so the static site and a checkout without the sibling tool folders list the teams too; a sibling checkout, when present, wins. Both SNES games use the team `.snespal` files from the NHLPA 93 SNES disassembly. Switching games keeps the same city and side, stays on an SPA or SPF of the same name when the new game has one, and stays on Images when that tab is open.
- **NHL 94 (PC)** lists 876 player frames from the sequential PPV sheets (`000_049.PPV` … `850_875.PPV`) and rink/crowd from `F000_049.PPV` and siblings. Every other `QPP` is a picture. The Images tab Ice rink is `RINK.TIL` + `RINK.MAP` (384×592, LoadRink blit) — 94 PC has no `RINK.QFS`. These files are **not** NHL 95 SHPI/RefPack: a PPV is a Mode X planar PackBits bank, and a QPP wraps that bank in Huffman `30 FB` or BTree `46 FB` (`lib/pc94-shape.js`, `lib/pc94-pack.js`). Copy the install into `pc94/` (or set `NHL_PC94_DIR`) and restart the server, or drop the folder on the page. SPA is the animation table in `HOCKEY.EXE` at file offset `0x358A4` (PC `updateanim` layout: flags word first, bit 15 loops). Stored frame numbers are 0-based, so the catalog adds one and they line up with Genesis. The Audio tab plays AdLib `.KMS`/`.CFG` songs, PCFF digital SFX, and announcer speech from `XBRUCE2.VIV` / `XRBARR2.VIV`. Jersey bins are the same 448-byte records as 95 PC, with 26 teams. Player-frame hotspots come from the planar bank header (`hotX`/`hotY`), not a 95-style GetHot table.
- **NHL 95 (PC)** lists the DOS sprite ranges (frame 1 is docs index 0, through frame 1134). SPF browses those ranges. SPA is the animation table in `HOCKEY.EXE` (file offset `0x110471`, the same layout `updateanim` walks). Stored frame numbers are 0-based, so the viewer adds one and they line up with Genesis. The 119 names are the C strings at file offset `0x109030`. Copy the game's `PPV` and `QFS` files into `pc95/` and restart the server, or start it with `NHL_PC95_DIR=/path/to/NHL95` (`NHL_PC94_DIR` for NHL 94) to read an installed folder in place. Player sprites are the sequential sheets `000_049.PPV`, `050_099.PPV`, through `950_999.PPV`, then `10001049.PPV`, `10501099.PPV`, and `11001149.PPV`. Tag `0000` in the first file is frame 1, and each later file continues that count. Rink and crowd sprites are the same kind of series: `F000_049.PPV`, `F050_099.PPV`, `F100_149.PPV`, plus the wider `F000_149.PPV`. Choose **Rink and crowd** in the Bank menu on the Frame tab. A file named `sprites.ppv` or `players.ppv`, or anything under `pc95/sprites/`, still fills the player bank. Every other `QFS` is a picture. Numbered runs such as `CREDIT00.QFS`–`CREDIT19.QFS` and `PORTR000.QFS`–`PORTR023.QFS` play as one picture. QFS is EA RefPack (`10 FB`), the same unpacker as `sub_97CE0` in NHL95PC. A PPV file, and the bytes inside a QFS, are an SHPI bank. The size, count, and offsets are little-endian, which is how `sub_B30B4` reads them. Each entry is a SPIT shape: type 123 is raw pixels, and the other types use the same run as `SPIT-To-BMP`. SPIT bytes 8 and 10 are the console 0, 0 point (`DrawSprite` subtracts them). The hotspot is `GetHot`'s HotList, with Y flipped so it sits on the same point as Genesis. `HOMEPALS.BIN` and `AWAYPALS.BIN` supply the 28 team palettes (Buffalo is team 1). Player and goalie frames use the `createPal` from `PAL-To-ACT`: `rinkpalShadowfixed` merged with the 64 team colors, the 128 mapping bytes at team offset 320, then `createjerseyShadowfixed` for colors 0–143. The referee, puck, goal light, and the rink bank use the game palette (`rinkpal` plus the team colors, keeping rink colors 251–255) so the shadow fix does not recolor them. Pictures keep the `!pal` stored with them. City titles take `CTBKGD.QFS` `!pal`, the same palette `TonightsGameScreen` loads before those shapes. The player stat bar takes `PREZ2.QFS` `!pal`. Team emblems take `EMBPAL.QFS`. Jumbotron clips and the scoreboard open on the team palette. On sprites the blitter skips index 255, and index 0 is equipment black. Pictures draw every index. The Audio tab plays the same EACSNDF `.KMS`/`.CFG` AdLib songs and PCFF digital SFX as NHL 94 PC, plus `XBRUCE2.VIV` announcer clips (OpenSpeechBank directory, BTree/RefPack then delta PCM) and loose `.IFF` 8SVX samples.

`data/frames.asm` is from NHL92Genesis. `frames93.asm`, `frames94.asm`, and `frames95.asm` are from the matching Genesis disassemblies. `frames95pc.asm` is the NHL 95 DOS sprite index from `Docs/Sprites/Readme.md`. `frames93snes.asm` is generated from NHLPA93SNES. `frames94snes.asm` is generated from the animation tables in the NHL 94 SNES ROM; sequences whose vblank list matches NHLPA 93 SNES keep that name, and the rest are `SPAextra`. Goalie poses on that ROM sit in `SPFlater` rather than the Genesis goalie names. The NHL 94 SNES rink reuses the NHLPA 93 SNES rink palette. `node test.js` checks the parsers and checks every NHL 92 frame against BMPs written by `ANIM-To-BMP`.

`node test.js` is change-aware: it diffs the branch against `origin/main` (plus uncommitted and untracked files), maps each changed file to the tests it can affect with the rule list in `test/select.js`, and prints what it selected and why. Docs (`*.md`, `docs/`) and `.github/` select nothing. `lib/anim.js` and the other parser and image libraries or `data/` select the core checks (`test/core.js`). `lib/audio/**` selects the sound suites, with narrower sets for the 68000, Z80 and SPC700 cores. `public/**` selects the browser scripts. A shared file (`test.js`, `test/select.js`, `package.json`, `server.js`, `lib/games.js`, `public/games.json`, ...) or a file no rule names runs everything. `node test.js --all` runs everything; `--base <ref>` diffs against another ref. When Playwright is installed, the browser group includes `test/browser/load-errors.js`. It opens every game in `public/games.json` in every mode, at desktop width and at 390 px with touch, and fails on page errors, console errors, or error text in the page such as "is not defined". This matters because the viewer shows a failed load in the caption instead of raising it. Set `NHL_SKIP_BROWSER=1` to skip the browser scripts. `node test.js --quiet` (or `-q`) hides the progress lines and prints a single summary line, or the full log if anything fails. On GitHub, every pull request runs the change-aware selection against its base branch, with Playwright (`.github/workflows/tests.yml`); running that workflow by hand runs `--all`. The Pages deploy on `main` runs only the tests the pushed commits affect (diff vs the previous `main`). The full suite runs only when you ask for it with `node test.js --all`. `node test/browser/editor-scrub.js` checks the sequencer's scrub bar in all five sound games and runs on its own. Without a sibling `ANIM-To-BMP` checkout, the dev server serves the NHL 92 banks bundled in `test/fixtures/ANIM-To-BMP`, the same files GitHub Pages ships.

## Maps editor (Genesis `.map.jim`)

`jim.html` (the **Image Editor** button) opens, paints, and exports Genesis `.map.jim` files: raw 92G tiles or 93G/94G `DecompressBytecode` packed tiles (same container; bit 15 of the tile-count word). It also opens NHLPA 93 SNES `.map.sjim` screens (one packed stream of map, BGR555 palettes, and planar tiles) and NHL 94 SNES screens (separate tiles/map/palette blobs packed LZ / Huffman / RLE; export is a ZIP of those files). The Images tab **Edit in Image Editor** button sends a catalog JIM, 93S screen, or 94S screen into the same page. Palettes, H/V flip, priority, and tile indexes round-trip. 90°/270° bake new tiles (the VDP name table has no rotate bit). Color remap, `printa`, and the `dobitmap` palette mask are preview-only. Portraits encode `UnpackPicture` 3-byte rows (3 bits + 5). Nothing is written back into a ROM. Spec: [`docs/nhl9x-studio-requirements.md`](docs/nhl9x-studio-requirements.md). Chip lab stays at `chip-lab.html` (direct URL; not in the header).

## Jersey editor (NHL 94 PC / NHL 95 PC)

`jed.html` (the **Jerseys** button) is a JED-style editor for `HOMEPALS.BIN` and `AWAYPALS.BIN`. Open a game folder, the two files (JED's `.O95` originals work too), or, under `node server.js`, the `pc94/` / `pc95/` install, which loads by itself (NHL 95 PC first; with both, the NHL 95 PC / NHL 94 PC switch picks). Pick a team (↑ ↓) and side (← →), then edit any of the 64 team colours with the sliders, numbers (6-bit VGA, 0–63) a #RRGGBB hex box or the colour picker (both snapped to 6-bit); the selected colour shows as 6-bit and 8-bit RGB and swatch tooltips appear instantly. The jersey spots list shows the 48 jersey zones with the colour each points at. With **Click paints spots with the selected colour** on (default), clicking the big sprite, a crest pixel or a spot points it at the selected colour (JED's painting); off, clicking selects the colour it uses. **Export file** writes the whole file (byte-identical when unedited), **Export / Import .bin** one 448-byte team record (192-byte colour-only records import too), **Export / Import .ACT** a 768-byte Adobe Color Table (772 with trailer) in sprite-colour space: entry *i* is the colour raw sprite pixel *i* shows, so loading it on a raw sprite exported from the game (EA-NHL-Tools `PPV-To-BMP`) in Photoshop gives the team's colours. It is the `createPal` that `PAL-To-ACT/binToAct.js` writes, byte for byte: `rinkpalShadowfixed` 0–127 and 251–255 plus the 64 team colours × 4 at 128–191 make the game palette; sprite colours 144–250 are that game palette at the record's mapping bytes (offset 320, sprite colours 128–255); `createjerseyShadowfixed` supplies 0–143 and 251–255 (the record's low-half map and skin entries are not used, as in the tools and the Studio). Import reads each team colour from the lowest sprite index 144–250 that maps to it (÷ 4, rounded), ignores the other entries, and lists any colour whose sprite indices disagree. The base ACTs ship in `public/jed-pals/`. Editing: **Undo** / **Redo** (Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Shift+Z / Ctrl+Y) step back through colour edits, spot reassignments, imports and reverts. The spots list groups dark / medium / light of a zone in one row, above them the 5 × 4 **jersey crest** picture and, for NHL 95 PC when `CRESTS4.PPV` is in the game files, the team's **scoreboard crest** (32 × 24 SHPI shape tagged with the team code, drawn as `DrawHudPanel` does: sprite colours 197–202, 213–218, 229–234, 245–250 through the team's record map, pixel 67 shown as game colour 103, 255 transparent), updated live. Hovering a spot outlines its pixels on the big frame (toggle); hovering the big frame outlines the spot under the cursor and clicking gives that spot the selected colour, as in JED. **Export / Import homepals.bin** (or awaypals.bin, following Home / Away) is the whole file; **Export / Import jersey** is the team on screen as one record, named like `CGY_away.bin`. The layout fills the window: the big frame (default frame 369, scale 1–30×, default 13×) takes the free space and the frame grid sits under a draggable splitter (its height is remembered). The preview uses the same palette (static frames such as the referee use plain `rinkpal`, like the Studio), shows the selected frame with as many neighbouring frames either side as fit (click one to select it), and a scrollable grid of every frame plus a frame number, slider and ◀ ▶.

Format (`lib/pc-jersey.js`): one 448-byte record per team, no header. NHL 95 PC: 28 records (12,544 bytes; 26 teams, then West and East All-Stars). NHL 94 PC: 26 records (11,648 bytes; no Anaheim or Florida). Record: bytes 0–191 are 64 colours (6-bit RGB, game palette 128–191); 192–319 map sprite indices 0–127 to the game palette (identity except blade side 8 → 16 and shadow 82 / 88 → 99); 320–447 map sprite indices 128–255 (skin 128–143, jersey zones 144–191, crest rows 192–255).
